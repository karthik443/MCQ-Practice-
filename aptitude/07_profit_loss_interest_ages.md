# 07 – Profit & Loss, Simple/Compound Interest, Ages

> **One-line idea:** profit/loss is a percentage change on **cost price (CP)**; discount is a percentage
> change on **marked price (MP)**; interest is a percentage change on the **principal**. Always identify the base.

---

## PART A – Profit, Loss & Discount

### A.1 Core formulas
- Profit = SP − CP; Loss = CP − SP.
- **Profit % = Profit/CP × 100**, **Loss % = Loss/CP × 100** (always on CP unless stated).
- SP = CP × (1 + p/100) for profit, CP × (1 − l/100) for loss.
- CP = SP × 100/(100 + p) or SP × 100/(100 − l).
- Discount = MP − SP; **Discount % = Discount/MP × 100**; SP = MP × (1 − d/100).

### A.2 Patterns
**1. Basic profit %**
CP = ₹500, SP = ₹600 → profit 100 → 100/500 = **20%**.

**2. Mark-up then discount**
**Idea:** use CP = 100.
Marked 20% above CP → MP = 120. Discount 10% → SP = 108 → **profit 8%**.

**3. Successive discounts**
Discounts a% and b% → single discount = a + b − ab/100.
20% and 10% → 20 + 10 − 2 = **28%**. (Not 30%, because the second discount is on the reduced price.)

**4. Same SP, same x% gain and loss**
Two articles sold at ₹990 each, one at 10% gain and one at 10% loss.
**Always a loss of x²/100 %** → 10²/100 = **1% loss** overall.
Check: CP1 = 990/1.1 = 900, CP2 = 990/0.9 = 1,100; total CP 2,000, total SP 1,980 → loss 20 = 1%. ✔

**5. Profit % on SP vs CP**
Profit of 20% *on selling price*: SP = 100, profit = 20, CP = 80 → profit on CP = 20/80 = **25%**.

**6. Dishonest shopkeeper (false weight)**
Sells 900 g as 1 kg at cost price → gain = (error/true weight sold) = 100/900 = **11.11%**.

**7. Finding the CP from two scenarios**
"Gain 10% at SP₁ ... loss 10% at SP₂" → difference in SP corresponds to 20% of CP.
Example: selling at ₹550 gives 10% profit, selling at ₹450 gives a 10% loss → CP = 500.

## PART B – Simple & Compound Interest

### B.1 Simple Interest (SI)
- **SI = P × R × T / 100** (T in years). Amount A = P + SI.
- Interest is **linear in time** (same each year).
- Time to double (SI): 100/R years.

**Example:** SI on ₹5,000 at 8% for 3 years = 5,000 × 8 × 3/100 = **₹1,200**.

### B.2 Compound Interest (CI)
- **A = P(1 + R/100)ⁿ**, CI = A − P. Interest in each year is earned on the *previous year's total*.
- Half-yearly compounding: rate R/2, periods 2n. Quarterly: R/4, 4n.
- **CI − SI for 2 years = P(R/100)²**; for 3 years add 3P(R/100)² + P(R/100)³ approx.

**Examples:**
- CI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years: A = 10,000 × 1.21 = 12,100 → CI = **₹2,100** (SI would be ₹2,000, difference 100 = 10,000 × 0.01 ✔).
- ₹8,000 at 10% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1 year: 8,000 × 1.05² = **₹8,820**.

### B.3 Other typical questions
- **Equal instalments**, "sum becomes double in n years at CI" – use the rule of 72: years ≈ 72/R.
- **Difference between CI and SI is given** → use P(R/100)² to find P or R.
- **Interest rate unknown**: A/P = (1 + R/100)ⁿ → compare with options.

## PART C – Ages

### C.1 Method
1. Let present ages be the variables (x, y or ratio parts like 3x, 5x).
2. Write each sentence as an equation (add/subtract years from each person).
3. The **difference** between two ages never changes; the **sum** changes by (years × number of persons).

### C.2 Examples
**Example 1:** father is 3 times son's age; after 10 years he'll be twice the son's age.
3x + 10 = 2(x + 10) → x = **10** (son); father = 30.

**Example 2:** ratio of ages of A and B is 3:5; after 6 years it becomes 2:3.
(3x + 6)/(5x + 6) = 2/3 → 9x + 18 = 10x + 12 → x = 6 → A = **18**, B = 30.

**Example 3:** average age of 5 members is 24 now. What was it 4 years ago? Each was 4 years younger → average **20** (the average shifts by the same years).

---

## Traps
1. Profit% is on **CP**; discount% is on **MP**. Mixing them is the #1 mistake.
2. Successive discounts can't be added.
3. Compound interest: be careful whether compounding is annual, half-yearly, or quarterly.
4. Age problems: add years to *every* person, including the unknown.

## Practice MCQs

1. CP ₹500, SP ₹600. Profit%?
   a) 10%  b) 16.67%  c) 20%  d) 25%
2. A shopkeeper marks 20% above CP and gives 10% discount. Profit%?
   a) 10%  b) 8%  c) 12%  d) 6%
3. SI on ₹5,000 at 8% p.a. for 3 years?
   a) 1,000  b) 1,200  c) 1,400  d) 1,500
4. CI on ₹10,000 at 10% for 2 years?
   a) 2,000  b) 2,100  c) 2,200  d) 2,500
5. Two articles sold at ₹990 each; one at 10% gain, other at 10% loss. Net result?
   a) no loss no gain  b) 1% loss  c) 1% gain  d) 2% loss
6. Father is 3× son's age. After 10 years he'll be 2× son's age. Son's age now?
   a) 8  b) 10  c) 12  d) 15
7. Successive discounts of 20% and 10% equal a single discount of?
   a) 30%  b) 28%  c) 25%  d) 72%
8. A sum doubles in 8 years at simple interest. Rate of interest?
   a) 10%  b) 12.5%  c) 15%  d) 8%
9. CI minus SI on a sum for 2 years at 10% is ₹50. The sum?
   a) 5,000  b) 4,000  c) 2,500  d) 1,000
10. A dishonest dealer uses a 900 g weight for 1 kg and sells at cost price. Gain%?
    a) 10%  b) 11.11%  c) 9%  d) 12.5%

<details><summary><b>Answers with solutions</b></summary>

1. **c.** 100/500.
2. **b.** 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08.
3. **b.** 5000 × 8 × 3/100.
4. **b.** 12,100 − 10,000.
5. **b.** x²/100 = 1%.
6. **b.** 3x + 10 = 2x + 20.
7. **b.** 20 + 10 − 2.
8. **b.** SI = P in 8 years → R = 100/8 = 12.5%.
9. **a.** P × (10/100)² = 50 → P = 50/0.01 = 5,000.
10. **b.** 100/900.
</details>
