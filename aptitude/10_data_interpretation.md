# 10 – Data Interpretation (Tables, Bar/Line/Pie Charts, Caselets)

> **One-line idea:** DI is not hard maths – it's **careful reading + fast approximation**. Read the question
> first, pull only the numbers you need, and estimate with round figures (the options are usually far apart).

---

## 1. Formulas you will use again and again

| Need | Formula |
|---|---|
| % change (growth) | (new − old)/old × 100 |
| Share of a part | part/total × 100 |
| Average | total/count |
| Ratio of two items | item1 : item2 (simplify at the end) |
| Pie chart: angle → % | angle/360 × 100 (1% = 3.6°) |
| Pie chart: angle → value | angle/360 × total |
| % points vs %: | 20% → 25% is +5 percentage points but **+25 %** |
| Average growth (CAGR) | ((final/initial)^(1/n) − 1) × 100 |

**Approximation tricks:**
- 1/3 ≈ 33%, 1/6 ≈ 16.7%, 1/7 ≈ 14.3%, 1/8 = 12.5%.
- To compare fractions a/b and c/d, cross-multiply (ad vs bc) instead of dividing.
- If the options differ by > 5%, rounding each number to 2 significant figures is safe.

## 2. Reading each chart type

### 2.1 Tables
Check: row/column headings, units (thousands, lakhs, crores, ₹, %), the year or category you're asked about.

### 2.2 Bar graphs
- Check the **axis scale** (does it start at zero? what is one gridline worth?).
- Grouped bars usually compare categories; stacked bars show part-of-whole.

### 2.3 Line graphs
- Steepness = rate of change. The question usually asks for "maximum increase/decrease between consecutive years".
- Compare **differences** for absolute change and **ratios** for % change.

### 2.4 Pie charts
- Sectors are proportional to angles, so you rarely need absolute values.
- When two pie charts are given, compare quantities only after converting each % into the actual value of its total.

### 2.5 Caselets (paragraph data)
Convert the paragraph into a table first. Write ratios as parts of a total.

## 3. Question patterns with worked examples

Use this dataset (sales in thousand units):

| Year | Product X | Product Y |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| 2022 | 200 | 150 |
| 2023 | 250 | 180 |
| 2024 | 300 | 270 |

1. **% growth:** X 2022 → 2023 = (250 − 200)/200 = **25%**.
2. **Average:** average yearly sales of X = (200 + 250 + 300)/3 = **250**.
3. **Share:** X's share in 2024 = 300/(300 + 270) = 300/570 ≈ **52.6%**.
4. **Compare growth:** Y: 2022→23 = 30/150 = 20%; 2023→24 = 90/180 = **50%** → the second step is higher.
5. **Ratio of totals:** Y total = 600, X total = 750 → **4 : 5**.
6. **Missing value:** if X + Y in 2025 is 640 and X is 360 → Y = 280.
7. **Percentage difference between two quantities:** Y in 2024 is 270 and X is 300. X is (300 − 270)/270 = 11.1% more than Y; Y is 30/300 = 10% less than X (base changes!).

**Pie chart example:** monthly expenses of ₹60,000: Rent 90°, Food 72°, Travel 54°, Others rest.
- Rent = 90/360 = 25% = ₹15,000.
- Food = 72/360 = 20% = ₹12,000.
- Travel = 54/360 = 15% = ₹9,000.
- Others = 360 − 216 = 144° = 40% = ₹24,000.

## 4. Traps
1. Units! (thousands vs. lakhs; % vs. absolute).
2. Percentage **points** vs **percent** change.
3. Using the wrong base when "A is x% more than B" (base = B).
4. Reading the wrong year/legend colour.
5. Taking totals from a stacked bar chart incorrectly (the top of the stack includes lower segments).

## 5. Practice MCQs (using the table above)

1. % growth of X from 2022 to 2023?
   a) 20%  b) 25%  c) 30%  d) 50%
2. Average yearly sales of X?
   a) 225  b) 250  c) 260  d) 275
3. In 2024, X's share of combined X + Y sales (nearest %)?
   a) 50%  b) 53%  c) 56%  d) 60%
4. For Y, which step had the higher growth %?
   a) 2022→2023  b) 2023→2024  c) equal  d) cannot say
5. Ratio of total Y sales to total X sales?
   a) 3:4  b) 4:5  c) 3:5  d) 2:3
6. In a pie chart, a sector is 72°. If the total is 5,000, its value?
   a) 500  b) 1,000  c) 1,200  d) 720
7. Y's growth from 2022 to 2024 (percent)?
   a) 60%  b) 70%  c) 80%  d) 90%
8. In 2023, Y is what % of X?
   a) 62%  b) 68%  c) 72%  d) 75%
9. A pie chart shows total expenditure ₹80,000. Food is 90°. Food expenditure?
   a) 15,000  b) 20,000  c) 25,000  d) 30,000
10. Production went from 200 to 260 in a year. Percent increase?
    a) 20%  b) 25%  c) 30%  d) 35%

<details><summary><b>Answers with solutions</b></summary>

1. **b.** 50/200 = 25%.
2. **b.** 750/3 = 250.
3. **b.** 300/570 ≈ 52.6% ≈ 53%.
4. **b.** 20% vs 50%.
5. **b.** 600:750 = 4:5.
6. **b.** 72/360 = 1/5 → 1,000.
7. **c.** (270 − 150)/150 = 120/150 = 80%.
8. **c.** 180/250 = 72%.
9. **b.** 90/360 = 1/4 → 20,000.
10. **c.** 60/200 = 30%.
